1. Areas and lanes of one layer MOCK-UP

Two neighbouring areas share one edge. Drag it, or a corner where edges meet, and every area that uses it follows. A block (dash-dot border) is a set of parts that belong together: drag its border and its parts come along. A lane says where its nets should run. Both are rough: a trace may leave them, and nothing here stops routing.

6. Colour modes compared MOCK-UP


Copper already has a colour per layer, so an overlay must not look like copper and must still say its layer. Differences are OKLab distances times 100, computed here for this board; larger is easier to tell apart. Colour-vision simulation after Machado, Oliveira and Fernandes (2009), full severity. No pass mark is set: these are numbers to compare.




Recommended: 1, Layer pastel. The pastel of the layer's own colour, with lightness steps so that two neighbours never share one; the function is said by the letter and the name. Copper is greyed while the plan is shown. In the product a key would show mode 2 for the one layer in view. Colour alone never carries the layer: in the peeling view the hatch direction and the name do too, because two of the layer colours look almost alike to protan and deutan vision in every mode that shows the layer.


7. A block on the schematic MOCK-UP


The same block on the sheet. Select the converter's symbols and choose Make block: the border is drawn around them, with the name. The parts are the members; wires and labels between them come along when the border is dragged or the block copied. Copy and paste makes new parts and a new, separate block, "Converter 2". Update PCB then offers to place it like Converter. Invented parts, names and positions.
